HISTORY OF COOKIES

According to historians, the earliest historical records of cookies indicate they were used as test cake, small portion of dough baked to check the oven's temperature.

The earliest biscuit style baked goods are believed to have originated in 7th century Persia. Persia was among the first regions to cultivate sugar, and a wide variety of cakes and pastries, in both large and small formats were already well known throughout the empire. Historians suggest that sugar originated in the lowlands of Bengal or elsewhere in Southeast Asia, from where it spread to Persia and subsequently to the eastern Mediterranean.

As the age of global exploration began, biscuits became an ideal provision, as they could remain fresh for long periods. For centuries, a specific type of "ship biscuit" a hard, iron like baked good was a staple on vessels departing from ports, as it could remain edible for months. In 17th and 18th century Europe, baking was a strictly regulated profession, overseen by various guilds or professional associations. To become a baker, one had to complete a multi year apprenticeship, progressing from apprentice to skilled worker, and finally to master baker.

Through these guilds, authorities could easily control the quantity and quality of baked goods. With the technological advancements of the 19th century Industrial Revolution, the capabilities of bakers expanded, allowing for the commercial production of a diverse range of biscuits both sweet and savory. Although a much wider variety exists today, the fundamental ingredients used to make biscuits remain unchanged. These ingredients include "soft" wheat flour, sugar, and fats, such as butter or oil.
